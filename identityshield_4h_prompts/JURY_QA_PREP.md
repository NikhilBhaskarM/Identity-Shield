# IdentityShield · Jury Q&A preparation (tough-judge edition)

Assume the judge is impatient, skeptical and a little angry. He has seen 40 projects today, most of them dashboards wrapped around ChatGPT. He wants to catch you bluffing.

**How to use this file:** each team member owns one section. Practise out loud, with someone playing the angry judge. Fill every `[X]` with your real numbers before the final round.

---

## 0. Survival rules (read first)

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Answer in **one sentence first**, then offer details: "Short answer: yes. Want the detail?" | Don't start with "So basically…" and a 2-minute story |
| Say **"I don't know, but here's how we'd find out"** | Never make up a number, a library feature or a test result |
| Admit a weakness, then show the mitigation | Don't argue, interrupt or get defensive |
| **Show it on screen** whenever you can | Don't say "it can do that" without being able to show it |
| Use **your real numbers** (from `docs/official/RESULTS.md`) | Don't quote sample-data metrics as real-world accuracy |
| Let the person who built that part answer | Don't let 4 people talk at once |
| Stay calm if he raises his voice: "Fair point. Here's the honest answer." | Don't apologize repeatedly or panic |

**Answer formula for hostile questions:** *Acknowledge → Answer honestly → Evidence (show it) → Limitation + next step.*

> Example: "Fair question. Yes, the sample data is generated by us. That's why we also ran on the official labeled data: F1 [X] vs the baseline's [Y]. The hidden-set score is what decides, and we didn't tune on it."

## 1. The "is this even real?" attacks

**Q1. "Every team says AI. Is this just ChatGPT with a dashboard?"**
No. There's no LLM in the detection path. Detection is five deterministic rule agents plus an Isolation Forest from scikit-learn, and scoring is a transparent formula. Show the Live scan: every step is real code with its JSON output.

**Q2. "You say 15 agents. These are just 15 Python functions. Why call them agents?"**
Honest answer: they're specialized, independent modules with their own input, output, permissions and status. They're not autonomous LLM agents. We call them agents because each has a single responsibility, its own least-privilege permissions, and can fail without stopping the others. Show the Agents page: each one reports its own state, timing and output.

**Q3. "This live scan looks fake. It's too slow to be real."**
The real scan takes about [X] seconds. We replay the **real trace** at a readable speed. Set the speed to **Instant** and look at the banner: "actual scan time [X] s". Show it.

**Q4. "Did you write this code or did the AI write it?"**
We used an AI coding assistant, like most teams today. We designed the architecture, the detection logic, the scoring formula and the contracts, and we reviewed and tested every phase: [X] automated tests pass. Then let the person who built a part explain it without notes. Practise this.

**Q5. "Show me it working on data you didn't prepare."**
Use the **Input data** page: let the judge type a username and two locations (presets help), then click **Scan input only**. Warn him first: "Two events can trigger impossible travel. Dormancy needs 30 days of history."

**Q6. "If I unplug the Wi-Fi, does it still work?"**
Yes, if you did Phase 27 (local vendor files). Emails go to the outbox in offline mode. If you didn't do Phase 27, say: "The UI libraries load from a CDN; offline mode is one step away."

## 2. Accuracy and metrics (he will push hardest here)

**Q7. "What's your accuracy?"**
Don't say "accuracy". Say: "On the official labeled samples, event-level F1 is [X], precision [X], recall [X], against the provided baseline's F1 of [Y]. Session-level alert precision is [X]."

**Q8. "Your sample data is generated by you. Your 100% is meaningless."**
Agree: "Correct, sample-data results only prove the pipeline works. That's why we measured on the official labeled data, and we didn't tune on the hidden set." If you did NOT run official data: "We haven't completed the official run yet; that's our top priority. The sample numbers aren't a performance claim."

**Q9. "How many false positives?"**
"[X] false-alarm sessions out of [Y] flagged on the labeled data." Show the Analytics page's False alarms column, and the controls that limit false alarms: confidence score, risk bands, friction budget and analyst feedback.

**Q10. "Why should I believe your baseline comparison?"**
"We used the organizers' baseline output, not our own." If you used the fallback baseline, say so: "We used a simple failed-login baseline because the official output [wasn't provided / had a different format]."

**Q11. "Did you overfit thresholds?"**
"We tuned only the session window and thresholds on the labeled samples, using a time-ordered split, and never on the hidden set. The thresholds live in one config file, so you can see them all."

**Q12. "What's your detection latency?"**
"p50 [X] ms, p95 [X] ms per event in our replay with batches of [N] events (`data/output/latency.json`)." If not measured: "We measured total pipeline time, [X] s for [N] events; a proper load-profile replay is implemented in `app/replay.py`."

**Q13. "Which is better, the ML or the rules?"**
Show the Analytics winner banner and agreement card, and say what it shows honestly, for example "Rules are more precise; ML caught [N] sessions the rules missed at the cost of [M] false alarms; the hybrid has the best F1." If ML adds nothing on the official data, say that too.

**Q14. "Why Isolation Forest? Why not deep learning?"**
"Little labeled data, mostly normal behavior, and a need for speed and explainability. Isolation Forest is unsupervised, fast and needs few parameters. Deep sequence models are future scope once there's enough data."

**Q15. "Your anomaly score: is it a probability?"**
"No. It's a normalized 0–1 anomaly score, not a calibrated probability. The risk score is a transparent weighted formula."

## 3. Detection logic deep-dives (expect "explain this line")

**Q16. "How does impossible travel work? What about VPNs?"**
"Haversine distance ÷ time between consecutive successful logins; above [900] km/h it's flagged, unless both logins come from the same network (ASN). VPNs can still cause false alarms; confidence, peer baselines and analyst feedback reduce them."

**Q17. "What if there's no geolocation in the logs?"**
"Then P1 can't fire for that event. We copy location from the matching Okta login when the IP matches; otherwise the field stays empty and other detectors still work."

**Q18. "How do you link an Okta login to AWS activity?"**
"Same identity, an Okta session started within [X] hours, and a matching source IP. Otherwise we group CloudTrail events by identity and access key with a 1-hour inactivity gap." Be ready for: "What if the IP changes?" "Then it becomes a separate session. That's a known limitation, and token-abuse detection covers part of it."

**Q19. "How do you detect privilege escalation chains?"**
"We map API calls to states such as CredCreate → PermGrant → RoleAssume → Impact, and flag 2 or more escalation states by one identity within 6 hours, keeping the ordered events as evidence." Show the attack path playback.

**Q20. "An admin does CreateAccessKey and AttachUserPolicy every day. Won't you flag them?"**
"The chain gets flagged, but the risk depends on context: privilege, sensitivity, and how unusual it is for that identity. Analyst false-positive feedback lowers the weight. A per-identity allowlist would be the next step." Be honest that admins can be noisy.

**Q21. "Dormant account: what if someone returns from a 2-month vacation?"**
"It's flagged at medium severity. On its own it lands in the step-up band, meaning MFA, not a block. Combined with other signals, it escalates."

**Q22. "How do you predict the next attack step?"**
"A first-order Markov model over escalation states, learned from labeled chains, with an expert prior when data is small." If challenged on accuracy: "It's a hint for the analyst, not an autonomous decision."

**Q23. "What does 'risk 94' actually mean?"**
"Weighted signal severities × privilege multiplier × resource sensitivity × a combination bonus for multiple patterns, capped at 100." Show the counterfactual table: "Without the escalation chain it would be [44]."

**Q24. "Why these weights? Did you just make them up?"**
"The starting weights are expert defaults in one config file, and analyst feedback adjusts them within bounds. On the labeled data we checked that they beat the baseline. Learned weights via the calibrator are an option if more labels exist."

**Q25. "What attacks will you miss?"**
Know your list: "Slow attacks spread over days beyond our window, attackers behaving exactly like the real user from the same IP and device, attacks only visible in logs we don't ingest (endpoint, network), and anything when geo data is missing."

## 4. Security of your own system (a cyber jury loves this)

**Q26. "Your tool can revoke sessions. What if someone hacks your dashboard?"**
"Revoke always needs analyst approval. Enforcement only goes through the simulator. There's an optional analyst token for every write action, and every action is hash-chained in the audit log." If you didn't build the token: "Authentication is the first production item."

**Q27. "Why didn't you revoke real AWS sessions?"**
"The hackathon rules forbid touching live cloud accounts or identity providers; everything goes through the supplied simulator. That's also the right design: high-impact actions need a human."

**Q28. "Can I tamper with your audit log?"**
"Each entry includes the previous entry's SHA-256 hash, so changing or deleting a row breaks the chain and Verify shows exactly where." Show **Verify**. If he says "I can just recompute all the hashes": "True, a local hash chain detects tampering but doesn't prevent a full rewrite by someone with database access. In production we'd anchor the head hash externally (write-once storage or a signed timestamp)." This honest answer scores well.

**Q29. "Logs are attacker-controlled. Can I inject something?"**
"Every value in the UI is HTML-escaped, including quotes in attributes. Email headers are stripped of CR/LF characters to block header injection. Uploaded input is only JSON-parsed, never executed, and filenames are sanitized."

**Q30. "Where are your secrets stored?"**
"SMTP password and tokens are in `.env`, which is git-ignored and never logged or returned by the API." Make sure that's actually true (`git status`).

**Q31. "Can I DoS you with a huge upload?"**
"Uploads are limited to 5 MB and 50,000 records per request. Pipeline runs are single-flight: a second run is refused with 409."

**Q32. "Is the dashboard authenticated?"**
If you skipped Phase 28: "No, it runs locally for the demo, and write actions need approval. Login is first on the production list." Don't pretend otherwise.

## 5. Architecture and engineering

**Q33. "Why FastAPI and plain JavaScript? Why no React?"**
"One process and no build step means fewer failure points in 8 hours. The UI is still responsive and componentized."

**Q34. "Will this scale to millions of events?"**
"Not as is. It processes in-memory batches on one machine, about [X] events/s measured. For production: streaming ingestion, a time-series database, and workers. The detectors are independent modules, so they parallelize." See `07_FUTURE_SCOPE.md`.

**Q35. "SQLite? Seriously?"**
"For a single-node demo, yes: zero setup and transactional. Alerts, weights, audit and notifications are stored there; the data access is isolated in one module, so swapping in Postgres is contained."

**Q36. "What happens if one agent crashes?"**
"It's recorded as failed in the trace, and the rest continue. If a whole re-run fails, the previous good results stay on screen." Show the test name, or the Agents page with a failed state if you can simulate it.

**Q37. "How did you test it?"**
"[X] automated tests: utilities, normalization, each detector, scoring bands, API contracts, email security, the background runner, threat audit and data input. Plus a smoke test and mock/live schema parity checks."

**Q38. "Did you test on Windows?"**
Answer from experience. The terminal scan enables ANSI colors and UTF-8 on Windows.

## 6. Product and business

**Q39. "Who would buy this? GuardDuty and Okta already exist."**
"They mostly see one side each. We join login and cloud activity into one explainable story per identity, with chain playback, counterfactuals and a friction budget. It's a lightweight open stack for SMEs and colleges that can't afford an enterprise SIEM plus a SOC team."

**Q40. "What's actually new here? Everything you listed exists somewhere."**
"No single piece is new; the combination is. Cross-platform correlation + chain reconstruction + next-step prediction + counterfactual explanations + graduated response with a friction budget, in one explainable, cheap tool." Don't claim "first in the world".

**Q41. "How do you stop alert fatigue?"**
"One alert per session, not per event. One email digest per run, deduplicated, with an hourly cap. Deduplicated audit entries. A friction budget for MFA prompts."

**Q42. "What would you do with 3 more months?"**
"Real-time streaming ingestion, production login with roles, external anchoring of the audit chain, more data sources (Entra ID, Google Workspace), learned weights from real analyst labels, and a pilot with one organization."

**Q43. "Privacy: you're storing user locations and activity."**
"Only fields needed for detection; the email summary has no raw logs or IPs. Production would add retention limits and role-based access, in line with Indian data-protection law (DPDP Act)." Don't go deeper than you know.

## 7. Gotcha and pressure questions

**Q44. "If your system flags the CEO and locks him out before a board meeting, who's responsible?"**
"It wouldn't lock him out: medium risk triggers step-up MFA, and revocation requires an analyst. The human stays in control."

**Q45. "Your demo data is designed to make you look good."**
"Yes, the sample scenarios demonstrate each attack type. That's why we show official-data metrics and let you enter your own events live."

**Q46. "Why should you win over the team that built a working mobile app?"**
"We solved the stated problem end to end: detection, correlation, explanation, response through the simulator, and measurement against the baseline. Everything you saw is real and inspectable."

**Q47. "What's the weakest part of your project?"**
Have one honest answer ready, for example: "Session correlation when IPs change," or "Weights are expert defaults, not learned from large real data." Judges respect this a lot.

**Q48. "Explain your project in one sentence."**
"IdentityShield detects attackers using stolen-but-valid credentials by joining Okta and AWS activity into one explainable story, scoring the risk, and responding with just enough friction, always with a human in control."

**Q49. Silence after your answer (an intimidation tactic).**
Don't fill it with rambling. Calmly ask: "Would you like me to show that on screen?"

**Q50. "I don't believe you. Show me the code."**
Have VS Code open with these files pinned: `app/agents/priv_escalation.py`, `app/decision/scoring.py`, `app/trace.py`, `app/audit.py`. The owner of each file explains it in 30 seconds.
